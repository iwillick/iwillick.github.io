Lab culture

The Willick Lab fosters a supportive, diverse environment that prioritizes well-being and work-life balance. Committed to rigorous, ethical, and impactful research, we view asking for help and acknowledging mistakes as essential to growth. Above all, we cultivate curiosity and camaraderie through open debate, shared expertise, and mutual gratitude.

The team

Ian Willick

Research interests: Plant responses to environmental stress, cold acclimation, ice nucleation, and plant nutrition.
I (he/him) use manipulative experiments to understand how plants tolerate or avoid injury in current and future environments. While much of my work has focused on how plants respond to low temperatures, my long-term goal is to understand how warming and water stress interact to affect plant physiology and nutrient availability in cool climates. I also investigate how much stress a plant can endure—and for how long—before it becomes lethal. Examining both the drivers of mortality and what promotes recovery after sub-lethal stress. Answering these questions identifies critical thresholds to avoid and meaningful traits to target for crop improvement.
